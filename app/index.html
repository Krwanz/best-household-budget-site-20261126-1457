      Savings goals

      Four goals, one emergency cushion first. Add money to a goal and watch its finish date move closer.

      Pay yourself first

      The Marlowe plan moves $1,200 to savings on payday, before any spending happens: $700 to the emergency fund, $250 to the holiday fund, $150 to the car fund and $100 to home repairs. That is why the emergency fund is on track to be full by March 2027 even though November is a tight month.

      The household budget guide

      The five habits behind this site — written for a real family keeping a real budget, not for a textbook.

      1. Give every dollar a job

      Income minus planned spending should be zero on paper before the month starts. If $400 is unassigned, it will be spent by accident; if it is assigned to savings, it will be saved on purpose.

      2. Pay yourself first

      Move savings on payday, not with what is left at month's end. The Marlowe household saves $1,200 of $5,815 — about 21% — before a single bill is paid.

      3. Track the small leaks

      Subscriptions, takeaway coffee and delivery fees are each small and together are $300 a month. Writing them down is the only way to see them.

      4. Keep one month of cushion

      An emergency fund of one month's essential spending (about $2,990 here) turns a broken washing machine into an annoyance instead of a debt.

      5. Review for 15 minutes a week

      Sunday evening, one coffee, one look at the dashboard: what went over plan, what can move, and what next week needs. A budget reviewed weekly stays honest.

      Groceries: the flexible giant

      At $620 planned and $512 spent so far, groceries are the household's biggest lever. A weekly meal plan and one big shop instead of five small ones typically saves a family like the Marlowes $60–$90 a month.

      Questions families ask

      What if income changes month to month?
      Budget from the lowest recent month, not the average. Freelance income of $480 is a bonus here, never the base the rent depends on.
      How much should go to fun?
      About 10% of income — here $608 across dining out, entertainment and personal spending. Zero fun fails by week three; planned fun lasts all month.
      Where does the leftover $1,137 go?
      At month's end it sweeps to the emergency fund. Leftovers are a result, not a plan — the plan already saved $1,200 on payday.
